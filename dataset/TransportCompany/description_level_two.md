A transport company wants to modernize its work. It wants to switch to a computerized solution. So the company's structure and organization were discussed in a meeting with the managing director. Here are the minutes:

I currently employ about 60 drivers. I also employ 6-7 other staff. These are planners and administrative staff. I need to store their names and social security numbers. Planners and administrative staff should be able to log into the system. They log in with a username and password. The administrative staff's field of activity is known. I also need to store my customers' details. The name and billing address are important. I also want to manage my vehicles. I have different types of vehicles. Examples are refrigerated trucks, small vans, loading platforms, and box trucks. For each vehicle, I want to store the license plate number and mileage. Sometimes a vehicle needs a repair or an inspection. For this case, I want to store the repair shop. The name and address of the repair shop are known. I also want to store the employee who took the vehicle there. And I want to store the duration and cost of the repair.

Each driver can be assigned orders. Usually a customer calls us. The customer says when they need a transport vehicle. The customer also says how big the package is. From this, the planner knows which type of vehicle the order needs. I always want to know which planner assigned an order to a vehicle. It must also be possible to save a comment with extra information about the order. I also need to know when the driver starts the order. I need to know when the driver finishes the order. I must always know which driver completed which order with which vehicle. For each order, there is always one customer. This customer is the client. Both the pick-up and delivery addresses are known. The order can be placed directly with an administrative employee. It can also be placed with a partner company. The name and address of the partner company are known.
