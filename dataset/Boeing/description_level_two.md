Boeing sells aircrafts to airline companies. Aircrafts are very expensive to build. For this reason, they are only built "on demand". This means a sales agreement is made with a customer first, before the airplane is actually built. (Demo versions of airplanes are an exception, but these are out of scope for this case.)

The sales are regulated through contracts with the airline companies. A single contract may consist of several acquisitions of airplanes. The global contract stipulates common elements across all acquisitions, such as delivery conditions, legal aspects, etc. Each acquisition of an airplane has further specific details. These include the chosen model of airplane, the negotiated price for that airplane, chosen options and customizations, the delivery date, and so on.

Each contract is managed by a Boeing salesperson. An employee can act as salesperson for several contracts. Contracts run for a long term, so the assigned salesperson may change over time. However, Boeing ensures there is always a salesperson available for the client.

Some airlines are related to each other. For example, main airlines often have a low cost daughter airline company. Boeing therefore keeps track as much as possible of the mother-daughter relationships between airline companies. This lets them track whether sold aircrafts are shifted to partner airlines of the original buyer.
