HospitalHouseMD is a hospital. This hospital accepts patients with rare and often unexplainable symptoms. Each patient is assigned one or more doctors. These doctors try to make a diagnosis for the sick patient. For each illness, HospitalHouseMD keeps a database. This database holds all possible treatments. The treatments can be applied to any diagnosis. The treatments are then executed. The head doctor must keep everything under control. So the head doctor must approve the diagnosis made by another doctor. This approval must happen before any treatment can happen.

A treatment can go through three stages. The first stage is the weakest treatment. In this first stage, no consent is needed from the patient. In this first stage, treatment can start immediately. Sometimes the first stage does not work. Then the treatment must move to stage 2. The treatment may also move directly to stage 3. This follows the rules described below. Moving a treatment from stage 1 to another stage needs the patient's consent. The patient must sign a form. This form gives consent to move the patient's treatments to a higher stage. Once the patient gives this consent, any applied treatment for the patient can move to a higher stage. No new consent is needed then. So the patient signs consent once. After that, all treatments can freely upgrade from stage 1 to stage 2 and stage 3. This continues until the patient retracts the consent.

Some patients have a treatment in stage 3. These patients require a higher follow-up rate. Nurses and doctors check these patients more often. Sometimes a specific treatment for a patient no longer needs the higher follow-up rate. Then the treatment can downgrade again to lower stages. Downgrading is the responsibility of the doctor.

Illnesses also get a priority score. The priority score is needed to prevent outbreaks. Such outbreaks can come, for example, from unknown viruses. The priority score of an illness can change at any time. For now, HospitalHouseMD uses only two priority scores: high and low. Sometimes an illness gets a high priority score. Then all applied treatments for that illness are immediately placed into stage 3. This happens through a priority stage upgrade. No patient consent is needed to move the treatment to stage 3. This applies when the illness has a high priority score.
