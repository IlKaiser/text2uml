The Team Sports Scouting System is used by several club employees. These employees include the head coach, the club director, and the scouts.

The head coach identifies player profiles for future signings. A profile includes target positions for a player. For example, GK means goalkeeper. LB means left back. A profile also includes other player attributes. Each attribute has a name and a value.

Scouts may note players on a long list. They can do this at any time. A scout notes a player if the player seems to match a target profile. From time to time, the head scout evaluates this long list. He does this when he sets up scouting assignments for his team. A scouting assignment lets the team investigate a specific player more thoroughly.

At the end of a scouting assignment, the scout submits a scouting report about the player. The report lists the pros of the player. It also lists the cons of the player. The report also gives a recommendation. For example, the player may be a key player. The player may be a first team player. The player may be a reserve team player. The player may be a prospective player. Or the player may be not a good signing.

The head coach and the head scout compare the first scouting results for a player profile. Then they decide which players to move to the short list. Each short-listed player can go through several more rounds of scouting. These rounds are part of scouting assignments. The head scout carries out some of these rounds himself. In the end, the head scout may recommend a player for signing. If he does, the director makes an official offer for the player.
