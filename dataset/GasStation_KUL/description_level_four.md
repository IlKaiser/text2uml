An oil company possesses gas stations. The gas stations spread across the country.

A customer can pay a refuel turn. The payment can use cash. The payment happens at the counter. A customer can pay a refuel turn. The payment can use a personal credit card. A customer can also ask for a refuel card.

A customer uses the refuel card. The customer does not pay the refuel turn immediately. Each month has an end. The fuel card has a holder. The holder receives a monthly invoice. The invoice lists the amounts. The amounts cover all refuel turns. The customer paid these turns. The customer used the fuel card.

The monthly invoice gives discounts. The customer has a turnover. This turnover falls in the invoiced month. The discounts depend on this turnover.

Invoicing and paying occur in any order. A customer can receive a second invoice. The customer may not have paid the first invoice yet.

Someone must first send each invoice. Only then can someone receive a payment.

Each refuel turn appears on only one invoice. The refuel turn happens in a month. The invoice belongs to that month.

A customer may not pay the invoice. The system keeps the unpaid refuel turns off the next invoice. Instead, the system sends the invoice again. This reminds the customer of the payment due.

A customer may repeatedly not pay the invoices. For example, the customer fails to pay two months in a row. The system then suspends the customer. The customer has a card. The system blocks this card. The card stays blocked. The customer must pay the bills first.

The exact threshold is not fully fixed. Very good customers get a bit more slack. New customers get less slack.

A customer may settle the bills. The customer may then return to the normal state.

Each gas station has pumps. Each pump contains gasoline. This gasoline has a particular type.

Only one customer can refuel a car at a given pump. This happens one at a time. A customer takes the nozzle. The refueling can then start. A customer puts the nozzle back. The refueling then stops. No other customer can use this pump between those two moments. A customer puts the nozzle back. The pump then becomes available for the next customer.

Each pump has a separate reservoir. The gasoline quantity may drop below a preset reordering level. Someone must then refill the reservoir. Refilling can occur at any time.

The system should produce a daily report. The report indicates which pump needs refilling. The report indicates the current gasoline level for each pump. The report indicates the quantity to order for each pump.

A tank bottom can contain dirt. The fuel may drop below a critical level. The system then automatically blocks the pump. No refueling can then occur with this pump.

A tank may still stay above the critical level. Someone refills that tank. The gas station responsible then temporarily blocks the pump. The block lasts approximately an hour. Dirt can then sink to the bottom.

The gas station responsible deems refueling with this pump safe again. The gas station responsible then releases the pump.
