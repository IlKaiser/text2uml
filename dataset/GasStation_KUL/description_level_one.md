An oil company owns many gas stations. The gas stations are spread across the country.

A customer can pay for a refuel turn in cash at the counter. A customer can also pay with a personal credit card. A customer can also ask for a refuel card.

With a refuel card, a customer does not pay the refuel turn right away. The customer pays later. At the end of each month, the holder of the fuel card gets a monthly invoice. The monthly invoice lists the amounts of all the refuel turns paid with the fuel card.

The monthly invoice gives discounts. The discounts depend on the turnover of the customer during the invoiced month.

Invoicing and paying can happen in any order. A customer can get a second invoice before paying the first invoice. But each invoice must be sent first. Only after sending an invoice can a payment be received.

Each refuel turn appears only on the invoice for the month of that refuel turn. A customer may not pay an invoice. Then the unpaid refuel turns are not added to the next month's invoice. Instead, the same invoice is sent again. The resent invoice reminds the customer of the payment due.

A customer may not pay the invoices again and again. For example, two months in a row. Then the customer is suspended. The customer's card is blocked. The card stays blocked until the bills are paid. The exact threshold to block a customer is not fully fixed. Very good customers get a bit more slack than new customers. If the bills are settled, a customer may return to the normal state.

Each gas station has many pumps. Each pump contains one particular type of gasoline. Only one customer can refuel a car at a given pump at a time. A customer takes the nozzle. Then the refueling can start. A customer puts the nozzle back. Then the refueling stops. Between those two moments, no other customer can use this pump. The nozzle is put back. Then the pump becomes available for the next customer.

Each pump has its own reservoir. The reservoir must be refilled when the amount of gasoline drops below a set reordering level. Refilling can happen at any time.

The system should produce a daily report. The report shows which pump needs to be refilled. For each pump, the report shows the current level of gasoline. For each pump, the report also shows the quantity to order.

The bottom of a tank can contain dirt. So the fuel may drop below a critical level. Then the pump is blocked automatically. Then no refueling can occur with this pump. A tank may still be above the critical level when it is refilled. Then the gas station responsible blocks the pump for about an hour. The block lets the dirt sink to the bottom. The gas station responsible judges when refueling with this pump is safe again. Then the pump is released.
