In hospitals, doctors prescribe examinations for patients. There are different types of examinations. Some examples are "cardiologic test", "echography", "radiology", and "scanner". Each examination has one type. Some types of examinations are not allowed for some hospitals. For example, some hospitals may not do NMR scans. A hospital can ask for permission to do a type of examination.

A hospital wants to make treatments better. The hospital also wants to watch the quality of treatments. So the hospital makes a treatment plan for each type of problem. A treatment plan lists the advised types of examinations. A patient comes with a problem. A doctor gives the diagnosis. This doctor works for the hospital that the patient visits. Then the doctor makes an actual treatment. The actual treatment is based on a treatment plan for the problem. The actual treatment can be different from the plan. The actual treatment can have advised examinations. The actual treatment can also have freely chosen medical actions. Examinations are not always at the same hospital. A doctor can send a patient to another hospital. The doctor does this when his own hospital has no permission for the examination. The hospital saves all this information. In this way, the hospital makes sure examinations are done only with permission. The hospital also watches how much actual treatments differ from the plans.
