In hospitals, doctors prescribe examinations for patients. There are different types of examinations, such as "cardiologic test", "echography", "radiology", "scanner", and others. Each single examination has only one type. Not all types of examinations are allowed for all hospitals. For example, some hospitals have no permission to perform NMR scans. A hospital can apply for permission for a type of examination.

Suppose a hospital wants to improve and monitor the quality of treatments. To do this, the hospital defines a treatment plan for each type of problem. A treatment plan lists the advised types of examinations. A patient comes to a hospital with a particular problem. A doctor affiliated to the visited hospital poses the diagnosis. The doctor then defines an actual treatment for the identified problem. The actual treatment is based on an existing treatment plan.

The actual treatment can deviate from the standard treatment plan. The actual treatment can include both advised examinations and freely chosen medical actions. Examinations do not have to happen at the same hospital. A doctor can send a patient to another hospital if the doctor's own hospital has no permission for the desired examination. By registering all this information, the hospital wants to ensure that examinations are only performed according to permissions. The hospital also wants to monitor how much actual treatments diverge from the advised treatment plans.
