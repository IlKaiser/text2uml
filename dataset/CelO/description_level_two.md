# Celebrations Organization System

The CelO application helps families and groups of friends organize birthday celebrations and other events. Organizers can track which tasks are complete. Organizers can also track who attends. Attendees can indicate what they are bringing to the event.

A small event usually has one organizer. Larger events need several organizers. An organizer provides a first and last name, an email address, a postal address, a phone number, and a password. The email address also serves as the username. The organizer also indicates the kind of event to be planned. The organizer selects the kind of event from a list of events. Examples include a birthday party or a graduation party. The organizer can instead create a new kind of event. The organizer must specify the event's start date/time and end date/time. The organizer must also specify the occasion and the location. The location can be chosen from a list. A new location can also be created. To create a new location, the organizer gives the location's name and address. The organizer then invites the attendees. The organizer enters each attendee's first and last names and email address. Sometimes an organizer only manages the event and does not attend the event. Sometimes an organizer also attends the event.

An attendee receives the email invitation. The attendee can then create an account. The attendee creates an account only if the attendee does not have one yet. The attendee sets a new password. The attendee's email address from the invitation becomes the attendee's username. Afterwards, the attendee can indicate whether the attendee will attend, may attend, or cannot attend the event. An organizer can view the invitation status of an event. For example, the organizer can see how many attendees have replied. The organizer can also see how many attendees have not yet replied. The organizer can also see who is coming for sure or may be coming.

An organizer selects an event. An event-specific checklist is then shown to the organizer. For example, a birthday party may have a task to bring a birthday cake. For each task on the checklist, the organizer can mark the task. The organizer marks the task as needs to be done, has been done, or is not applicable for the event.

An organizer can also add new tasks to the list. These new tasks then become available for the next event. For example, an organizer can add "bring birthday candles" to the list for a birthday party. This task is then available for the next birthday party too. An organizer can also assign a checklist task to attendees. For example, an organizer can indicate that an attendee should bring the birthday cake to the event. In this case, the list of tasks for attendees is shown. The list is shown to the attendees who have confirmed their attendance. An attendee can then select the attendee's own tasks. The organizer can then see who is bringing what to the event.
