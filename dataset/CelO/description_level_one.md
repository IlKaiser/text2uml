# Celebrations Organization System

The CelO application helps families and groups of friends. They can organize birthday celebrations and other events. Organizers can keep track of which tasks are done. They can also see who attends. Attendees can say what they are bringing to the event.

A small event usually has one organizer. A larger event needs several organizers. An organizer gives their first and last name. They give their email address. This email address is also their username. They give their postal address. They give their phone number. They give their password.

An organizer also picks the kind of event to plan. They choose from a list of events. For example, a birthday party or a graduation party. Or they create a new kind of event. The organizer must give the start date and time. They must give the end date and time. They must give the occasion. They must give the location. The location can be picked from a list. Or a new location can be created. To create one, the organizer gives its name and its address.

Then the organizer invites the attendees. The organizer enters each attendee's first and last name. The organizer also enters each attendee's email address. Sometimes an organizer only manages the event and does not attend. Sometimes an organizer also attends the event.

An attendee gets the email invitation. The attendee can create an account if they do not have one yet. They set a new password. Their username is the email address from the invitation. Then the attendee can reply. They can say they will attend. They can say they maybe will attend. They can say they cannot attend.

An organizer can view the invitation status of an event. For example, how many attendees have replied. How many have not yet replied. Who is coming for sure. Who maybe will be coming.

An organizer selects an event. Then a checklist for that event is shown to the organizer. For example, a birthday party may have a task to bring a birthday cake. For each task on the checklist, the organizer can set its state. The task needs to be done. The task has been done. Or the task is not applicable for the event.

An organizer can also add new tasks to the list. A new task will then be available for the next event too. For example, an organizer can add "bring birthday candles" to the list for a birthday party. This task will then be available for the next birthday party too.

An organizer can also give a task to attendees to do. For example, the organizer can say an attendee should bring the birthday cake. When this happens, the list of tasks for attendees is shown. It is shown to attendees who have confirmed they will attend. An attendee can then select their tasks. This way, the organizer can see who is bringing what to the event.
