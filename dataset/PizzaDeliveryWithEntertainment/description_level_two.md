One of your (hipster) acquaintances thinks he has the next billion-dollar startup idea for an app: Pizza Delivery with Entertainment. He heard that you are following the course on database management. So he asks you to design the UML class diagram. Afterwards, he will use this diagram to ask programmers to build the app.

He explains the basic functionality like this. Customers can order pizzas from restaurants. The pizzas are delivered to a specific address. If they want, customers can also choose a special "entertainment order". When an order is an entertainment order, the delivery person stays with the customer after delivering the pizza. He or she then entertains the customers for a certain amount of time, for example by singing, making jokes, or doing magic tricks.

Now follows a detailed explanation of what the app can do. People create an account and become app users. They must give their birthday, and fill in their name and address. Every user should also be uniquely identifiable.

Once the account is created, the users see three options. The first option is to select "business owner". Business owners must also provide their LinkedIn account, so we can add them to our profession network. Every business owner can own a number of pizza restaurants. For each pizza restaurant, we want to register the zip code, address, phone number, website, and opening hours.

Each pizza restaurant can offer a number of pizzas. For each pizza, we want to keep the name (margarita, quattro stagioni, etc.), the crust structure (for example, classic Italian crust, deep dish crust, or cheese crust), and the price. Two pizzas from different pizza restaurants may have the same name. But they are not exactly the same, because the taste is different. So each pizza should be considered unique. Pizzas should be distinguishable even if they have the same price. For example, a pizza margarita from Pizza Pronto in New York costs $12. A pizza margarita from Pizza Rapido in Singapore also costs $12. These two must still be distinguishable.

The second option is to select "hungry customer". For these hungry customers, we need a delivery address. Hungry customers can make orders for pizzas. Each order gets an ID. The app should log the date and time when the order was placed. We also let the hungry customer indicate the latest time of delivery. We ask how many people the order is for. An order can be for one or more pizzas.

A special type of order can also be made: the entertainment order. Not every order has to be an entertainment order. But sometimes a hungry customer wants to be entertained while eating the pizza. In that case, we register all the regular order information. We also register the type of entertainment the user requests, and for how long (a duration).

The third option is to select "entertainer". When users select entertainer, they must provide a stage name. They must write a short bio about themselves. They must also indicate their price per 30 minutes. Every entertainment order is fulfilled by exactly one entertainer. Every entertainer can choose which pizza restaurant(s) he or she wants to work for. For each pizza restaurant an entertainer wants to work with, he or she must indicate his or her availability by day (Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, etc.).
