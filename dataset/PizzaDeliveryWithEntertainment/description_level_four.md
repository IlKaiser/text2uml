He explains the basic functionality. Customers can order pizzas. The pizzas come from restaurants. The delivery brings the pizzas to a specific address. Customers can also choose a special "entertainment order". Customers may want this. Then they choose it. An order can be an entertainment order. Then the delivery person delivers the pizza. Now follows a detailed explanation. It describes the capabilities. People create an account. They become app users. They must indicate their birthday. They fill in their name. They fill in their address. Every user must be uniquely identifiable.

The users create the account. Then the users see three options. The first option is "business owner". We ask these business owners for their LinkedIn account. We add them to our professional network. Every business owner can own a number of pizza restaurants. Each restaurant has a zip code. We register it. Each restaurant has an address. We register it. Each restaurant has a phone number. We register it. Each restaurant has a website. We register it. Each restaurant has opening hours. We register them.

Each pizza restaurant can offer a number of pizzas. Each pizza has a name. We keep it. Examples are margarita and quattro stagioni. Each pizza has a crust structure. We keep it. Examples are classic Italian crust, deep dish crust, and cheese crust. Each pizza has a price. We keep it. Two pizzas may come from different pizza restaurants. They may have the same name. They will not be exactly the same. The taste will be different. So we consider them unique. Pizzas may have the same price. We must still distinguish them. For example, one pizza margarita comes from Pizza Pronto in New York. It costs $12. Another pizza margarita comes from Pizza Rapido in Singapore. It also costs $12. We must distinguish the two pizzas.

The second option is "hungry customer". These hungry customers have a delivery address. We need it. Hungry customers can make orders. The orders are for pizzas. Each order gets an ID. Our app logs the order date. Our app logs the order time. The hungry customer can indicate the latest time of delivery. We ask the number of people. The number is for the order. An order can be for one or more pizzas.

Customers can also make a special type of order. It is the entertainment order. Not every order must be an entertainment order. A hungry customer may want entertainment. He or she eats the pizza. Then we register all the regular order information. We also register the type of entertainment. The user requests this type. We also register the duration.

The third option is "entertainer". Users may select entertainer. Then they provide a stage name. They write a short bio about themselves. They indicate their price per 30 minutes. Exactly one entertainer fulfills every entertainment order. Every entertainer can choose the pizza restaurants. He or she will work for them. An entertainer wants to work with certain pizza restaurants. He or she indicates his or her availability. The availability is by day. This is for each restaurant. Examples are Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday.
