A city uses the Bus Transportation Management System (BTMS). The BTMS helps run the city's public bus system each day.

The BTMS stores a driver's name. The BTMS automatically gives each driver a unique ID. A bus route has a unique number, which city staff choose. A bus has a unique licence plate. The route number can be at most 9999. The licence plate can be up to 10 characters long.

City staff assign buses to routes up to a year in advance. Several buses may be assigned to one route in a day. Each bus serves at most one route per day. However, a bus may run different routes on different days.

City staff also post the schedule for bus drivers up to a year in advance. Each route has three shifts, which are a morning shift, an afternoon shift, and a night shift. City staff assign a driver to a shift for a certain bus on a certain day.

The BTMS gives city staff great flexibility. There is no limit on how many shifts a driver has per day. A driver can even work two shifts at the same time.

The current BTMS cannot update the details of drivers or buses. The BTMS can only add and delete drivers and buses. However, the BTMS can mark a driver as on sick leave. The BTMS can also mark a bus as in the repair shop. When a driver is on sick leave, the driver cannot be scheduled. When a bus is in the repair shop, the bus cannot be assigned to a route.

For a chosen day, the BTMS shows an overview. For each route number, the overview shows the licence plate of each assigned bus. The overview also shows the entered shifts. The overview shows the IDs and names of the assigned drivers. If a driver is currently sick, the BTMS highlights that driver in the overview. If a bus is in the repair shop, the BTMS highlights that bus in the overview.
