A city uses the Bus Transportation Management System (BTMS) to simplify the daily activities of the city's public bus system.

The BTMS records a driver's name and automatically gives each driver a unique ID. A bus route is identified by a unique number set by city staff. A bus is identified by the bus's unique licence plate. A route number can be at most 9999. A licence plate can be up to 10 characters long.

City staff assigns buses to routes for up to a year in advance. Several buses may be assigned to one route per day. Each bus serves at most one route per day, but each bus may serve different routes on different days.

In the same way, city staff posts the schedule for bus drivers for up to a year in advance. Each route has a morning shift, an afternoon shift, and a night shift. City staff assigns a driver to a shift for a particular bus on a particular day. The BTMS gives city staff great flexibility. There are no limits on how many shifts a driver has per day. A driver can even be assigned to two shifts at the same time.

The current version of BTMS cannot update the information of drivers or buses. The BTMS only supports adding and deleting drivers and buses. However, the BTMS can indicate whether a driver is on sick leave and whether a bus is in the repair shop. When a driver is on sick leave, that driver cannot be scheduled. When a bus is in the repair shop, that bus cannot be assigned to a route.

For a given day, an overview shows, for each route number, the licence plate of each assigned bus, the entered shifts, and the IDs and names of the assigned drivers. If a driver is currently sick, the sick driver is highlighted in the overview. If a bus is in the repair shop, the bus in the repair shop is highlighted in the overview.
