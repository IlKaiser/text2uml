Flander's bus company is named "De Lijn". De Lijn has a number of busses. The busses serve different bus lines. Each bus line has a different destination.

The company possesses a number of trajectories. Town authorities must approve each trajectory. The company asks for approval. The authorities may refuse the approval. This refusal has no appeal. The authorities may instead ask the company. The company must change the trajectory. The company may then ask again for approval. This procedure may repeat a number of times.

The authorities give approval. The company then includes the trajectory in its business. The next calendar year begins. The trajectory then becomes operational. From that time on, the company can assign busses to the trajectory. The trajectory may be suspended for some time. Many kinds of reasons cause a suspension. At a certain moment, the company cancels the trajectory.

Buses get a 'line' number. A 'Line' is mostly the same as a trajectory. A 'Line' is not entirely the same. We assume one thing for this case. A line groups a number of overlapping trajectories. Here is one example. A line has a last section. The bus may skip this section. This happens in the late evening. The trajectory is then shorter. Here is another example. There is a bifurcation at one end. The bifurcation has two different end points. Buses take the end points alternatively. Consider Leuven. Leuven has "Line 2 Campus". Leuven also has "Line 2 Boskant". These two lines follow partly the same route. They also follow partly different routes.

Bus company "De Lijn" wishes to test a new type of season ticket. This ticket is called MENSO. De Lijn assigns each bus line a category. This supports the MENSO project. A MENSO ticket is valid during one year. A trip has a price. The line has a category. The price depends on this category. You travel with that line. So De Lijn does not know the ticket price in advance. De Lijn invoices MENSO users each month. The invoice covers trips. These trips are from the past month.

The user pays for a trip with MENSO. The user enters the bus. The user introduces his or her card in the ticket machine. The ticket machine is next to the bus door. The system then registers the trip time. The system registers the trip date. The system registers the trip line. The user may forget his or her MENSO card. Then the user pays in the usual way. The user buys a ticket. Or the user uses another type of card.

The system collects the time information. The system collects the date information. The system collects the line information. The system uses this information. The system generates the monthly invoices. The invoice has a total amount. De Lijn grants the customer a discount. The discount is up to 20%. The discount depends on this amount. Invoicing happens in any order. Payment happens in any order. A customer might receive the next invoice. The customer has not yet paid the first invoice.

"De Lijn" hopes to gather interesting information. It uses the MENSO system for this. This information concerns the behaviour of its customers. The customer subscribes to MENSO. De Lijn then requests some personal data. This data includes the home address. This data includes the office address. This data includes the working hours. The MENSO card use also generates information. De Lijn combines this data with that information. De Lijn hopes to detect patterns in travelling habits. The bus lines have routes. These patterns let De Lijn optimise the routes. The bus lines have a frequency. These patterns let De Lijn optimise the frequency.
