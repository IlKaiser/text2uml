"De Lijn" is a bus company in Flanders. It has many buses. The buses run on different bus lines. Each line has a different destination.

The company owns many trajectories. Town authorities must approve each trajectory. The company asks the town authorities for approval. The authorities may refuse the approval. This refusal cannot be appealed. Or the authorities may ask the company to change the trajectory. Then the company can ask for approval again. This can happen many times.

Sometimes the authorities give approval. Then the trajectory becomes part of the company's business. It starts to operate at the start of the next calendar year. After that, buses can be assigned to the trajectory. The trajectory may be suspended for a while. This can happen for many reasons. At some point, the trajectory is cancelled.

Each bus gets a 'line' number. A 'Line' is mostly the same as a trajectory. But it is not exactly the same. In this case, a line groups several overlapping trajectories. For example, in the late evening, the last part of a line may be skipped. Then the trajectory is shorter. Or a line splits into two different end points. The buses take these two ends alternately. For example, in Leuven there is "Line 2 Campus" and "Line 2 Boskant". These follow partly the same route and partly different routes.

"De Lijn" wants to test a new kind of season ticket. It is called MENSO. For the MENSO project, every bus line gets a category. A MENSO ticket is valid for one year. The price of a trip depends on the category of the line you travel with. So the price of the ticket is not known in advance. MENSO users get an invoice at the end of each month. The invoice covers the trips of the past month.

Here is how a user pays for a trip with MENSO. The user enters the bus. Next to the bus door there is a ticket machine. The user puts the card into the machine. The system then registers the time, date and line of the trip. Sometimes a user forgets the MENSO card. Then the user must pay the usual way. The user buys a ticket or uses another type of card.

The system collects the time, date and line information. It uses this to make the monthly invoices. The customer may get a discount of up to 20%. The discount depends on the total invoice amount. Invoicing and payment can happen in any order. A customer might get the next invoice before paying the first one.

With the MENSO system, "De Lijn" hopes to learn interesting things about its customers. A customer subscribes to MENSO. The company then asks for some personal data. This data includes the home address, the office address and the working hours. De Lijn combines this data with the data from the MENSO card. It hopes to find patterns in travelling habits. This helps to improve the routes and the frequency of the bus lines.
