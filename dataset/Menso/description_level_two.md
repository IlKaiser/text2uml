The Flemish bus company "De Lijn" owns a number of busses. These busses serve different bus lines. The bus lines have different destinations. The company also owns a number of trajectories. Town authorities must approve each trajectory. The company asks for approval. The town authorities may refuse the approval without appeal. The town authorities may also ask the company to change the trajectory. The company may then ask for approval again. This procedure may be repeated several times. Sometimes approval is given. Then the trajectory becomes part of the company's business. The trajectory starts operating at the beginning of the next calendar year. From then on, busses can be assigned to the trajectory. The trajectory may be suspended for some time. This can happen for all kinds of reasons. At a certain moment, the trajectory is cancelled.

Busses receive a 'line' number. A 'Line' is mostly the same as a trajectory. But a 'Line' is not entirely the same as a trajectory. For this case, we assume one thing. A line groups a number of overlapping trajectories. For example, in the late evening the last section of a line may be skipped. Then the trajectory is shorter. Or a line may split into two different end points at one end. These two end points are used alternately. For instance, in Leuven there is "Line 2 Campus" and "Line 2 Boskant". These two lines follow partly the same route and partly different routes.

"De Lijn" wants to test a new type of season ticket. This ticket is called MENSO. For the MENSO project, every bus line has been assigned a category. A MENSO ticket is valid for one year. The price of a trip depends on the category of the line. Because of this, the ticket price is not known in advance. MENSO users are invoiced at the end of each month. This invoice covers the trips of the past month.

The user pays for a trip with MENSO. The user simply inserts the card into the ticket machine next to the bus door. This happens when the user enters the bus. The system then registers the time, date and line of the trip. Sometimes the user has forgotten the MENSO card. Then the user must pay in the usual way. The user buys a ticket or uses another type of card.

The time, date and line information is collected. This information is used to generate the monthly invoices. The customer receives a discount of up to 20%. This discount depends on the total invoice amount. Invoicing and payment happen in any order. A customer might receive the next invoice before paying the first invoice.

Through the MENSO system, "De Lijn" hopes to gather interesting information. This information is about the behaviour of its customers. The customer subscribes to MENSO. Then the customer is asked for some personal data. This data includes home address, office address and working hours. De Lijn combines this personal data with the information from the MENSO card. De Lijn hopes to detect patterns in travelling habits. These patterns allow De Lijn to optimise the routes and frequency of the bus lines.
