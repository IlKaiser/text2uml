This exercise teaches you to build your solution step by step. You re-evaluate your solution each time. This handles changing requirements.

First, create a class diagram for the use case in STEP 1. Add attributes. Add event types. Add non-default FSMs. Add constraints. Add these only if you need them. Revise your model at each step. Add elements where you need to. Remove elements where you need to. Change elements where you need to.

STEP 1
Your brand new company sells goods to local supermarkets. You want a simple information system. The system keeps track of your stock. The system keeps track of your customers. The system keeps track of their orders.

You register a new customer. You enter the customer's address. You also enter the customer's VAT number.

You want to keep track of your stock. So you want to register the products you offer. You also want to register the current stock of each product. Your customers place orders. Each order has one or more order lines. Each order line records the quantity ordered of one product.

You want to keep payments and deliveries simple. Your business is small. So your customers must pay for the order in full first. Then the order is sent out for delivery. Orders are always shipped in full. This keeps shipping simple. You want the information you need for your accounts. So you record when orders are paid for. You also record when orders are shipped.

Each working day ends. Then you adjust the stock levels of your products by hand. You adjust them in the system. You look at the orders placed that day. You use these orders to adjust the stock levels.

STEP 2

Your business grows. You start to offer more exclusive products. These products are not always available. You want to keep your customers satisfied. So you want to organise deliveries line by line. You can ship available products at once. You can ship unavailable products later.

You need to adapt your information system for this. The system must keep track of partially shipped orders. You want to record which order lines have been shipped. You also want to record which order lines are still on the to-be-shipped list. You also mark orders that have been fully shipped. Then you know you can remove these orders from your watchlist.

STEP 3

Your products become more popular. You start to supply larger supermarkets. These supermarkets sometimes have several warehouses. They use these warehouses for different locations. You want to keep track of the customer's head office address. You also want to keep track of the addresses where orders are delivered.

You want to make your business more professional. So you want to send invoices to your customers for their orders. Customers can now pay the invoice directly when they place the order. Or customers can pay after the order has been delivered. An order must still be paid in full.

STEP 4
Your business grows. Keeping track of shipments line by line becomes too hard. Instead, you want to do this for each group of order lines shipped together.

You have also noticed some problems with the delivery service you use. Customers have told you that some deliveries are missing. Some deliveries are also damaged. You want more information about the deliveries. Then you can decide something. You can switch to another delivery service. Or you can deliver the goods yourself. So you want to know the status of each delivery. Remember that partial deliveries of one or more order lines are possible. You track a shipment as it is packed. You track a shipment as it is shipped. You track a shipment as it is received by the customer.
