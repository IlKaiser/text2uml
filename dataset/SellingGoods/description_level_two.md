This exercise teaches you to build and re-evaluate your solution step by step. This way you can handle changing requirements. First, create a class diagram for the use case in STEP 1. Add attributes, event types, non-default FSMs and constraints if needed. For each step, revise your model. Add, remove or change elements where needed.

STEP 1
Your brand new company sells goods to local supermarkets. You want a simple information system. It keeps track of your stock, your customers and their orders.

When you register a new customer, you enter their address and VAT number.

To track your stock, you want to register the products you offer. You also want to register the current stock of each product. Each customer order has one or more order lines. Each order line records the quantity ordered of one product.

Your business is small, so you want to keep payment and delivery simple. Customers must pay for their order in full before it is sent out for delivery. Orders are always shipped in full. This keeps shipping simple. For your accounts, you want to record when orders are paid for. You also want to record when they are shipped.

At the end of each working day, you adjust the stock levels by hand. You base this on the orders placed that day.

STEP 2

Your business grows. You start to offer more exclusive products. These products are not always available. You want to keep customers satisfied. So you want to organise deliveries line by line. Available products can be shipped at once. Unavailable products can be shipped later.

You need to adapt your information system for partially shipped orders. You want to record which order lines have been shipped. You also want to record which lines are still to be shipped. Orders that are fully shipped will be marked. Then you know you can remove them from your watchlist.

STEP 3

Your products become more popular. You start to supply larger supermarkets. These supermarkets sometimes have several warehouses for different locations. You want to keep track of the customer's head office address. You also want to keep track of the addresses where orders are delivered.

You want to make your business more professional. So you want to send invoices to your customers for their orders. Now customers can pay the invoice directly when they place the order. Or they can pay after the order has been delivered. An order must still be paid in full.

STEP 4
Your business grows further. Tracking shipments line by line becomes too hard. You would rather track each group of order lines that are shipped together.

Also, you have noticed problems with the delivery service you use. Customers have told you that some deliveries are missing or damaged. You want more information about the deliveries. Then you can decide whether to switch to another delivery service or deliver yourself. To do this, you want to know the status of each delivery. Remember that partial deliveries of one or more order lines are possible. You want to track a shipment as it is packed, shipped and received by the customer.
