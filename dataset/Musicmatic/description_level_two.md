A group of students saw the success of Spotify. The students now want to build their own music streaming website, called Musicmatic. The students are economists, so the students do not know the details of databases. For this reason, the students have asked you to create a UML class diagram.

The Musicmatic website will offer a large number of songs. For each song, the system must store the song's title, year, length, and genre. The system will also store artist information. Artist information includes the date of birth, the name, and a URL to a website (for example, a Wikipedia page) with more details about the artist. You can assume that an artist is uniquely identified by the artist's name. You can also assume that a song always belongs to exactly one artist. The students point out that different songs may have the same title. Only the combination of song and artist is unique.

The database must also store information about the people who use Musicmatic. The students decided to separate users into just two types. Regular users can buy music. Business users deliver the content, which means business users upload the music. For each user, the system records a unique ID, a name, and an address. Business users also have a VAT number.

The students want a flexible service. The students decided that business users can only upload individual songs. Each uploaded song is classified as either a single or a hit. Regular users can buy singles directly. As an alternative, a regular user can compose an album made of multiple hits, but no singles. For each hit in an album, the database stores the hit's position as a track number. Note that a regular user's album can be turned into a suggestion for other regular users with similar purchasing behavior.

Finally, one user can be a regular user at some times, for example when downloading a single or an album. The same user can be a business user at other times, for example when uploading self-made songs to Musicmatic.
