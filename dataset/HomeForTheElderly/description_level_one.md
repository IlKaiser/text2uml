A Home for the elderly is divided into departments. Each department has a number of rooms. Each room has a number of beds. Each inhabitant is assigned one bed. The inhabitant uses this bed for the stay. Each bed belongs to one category. Each category has a price. The home offers different types of stays. A type of stay is the same as a category. The types are rest home (RH), rest & nursing home (RNH), and service flat (SF). One inhabitant can have more than one stay at the same time. Here is an example. John stays in a service flat. Then John breaks his leg. So John must stay in rest & nursing for a while. The service flat stay of John is then suspended. It is not ended. The switch to rest & nursing needs no proposal. John is already an inhabitant.

People who want to live in the home go through a registration process. In this process, the person answers a set of questions. These questions are about the basic identification data of the person. As a final step, the data of the person is registered in the system. Then the basic registration is done. Next, the abilities of the person are assessed. This is done by asking questions. These questions gauge how much help the person needs. The help is for basic activities. These activities are eating, dressing, and personal care. Personal care is, for example, taking a shower. This gives an indication of the type of stay best suited for the person. The assessment defines a type of stay for the person. Then the person has been assessed. The person is put on the waiting list for that type of stay. The position on the waiting list depends on the registration date only. A bed of the matching category becomes free. Then it is proposed to the person at the highest position on the waiting list. The person must then inform the Rest & Nursing Home within three days. The person tells them whether s/he accepts or refuses the proposal. The person may refuse the proposal. Then the person stays on the waiting list. A new proposal is made to the next person on the list. This next person requested this type of stay. This goes on until a person accepts the proposal. A proposal is accepted. Then the abilities of the person are re-assessed. This re-assessment validates the requested type of stay. After this validation, the date of intake is set. The stay starts on that day. Sometimes the type of stay must change after the re-assessment. In that case, the proposal is marked as invalidated. The requested type of stay is updated. But the person keeps the same position on the waiting list.

Stays are invoiced once a month. For the sake of simplicity, payments are not treated as objects. They are treated as events. A payment is received. Then it is registered. The outstanding amount of the invoice goes down by the amount paid. The remaining amount may be zero. Then the invoice is registered as "paid". The remaining amount may still be positive. Then the paid amount was too low. In that case, the outstanding amount is adjusted. The invoice is re-sent. The invoice shows the remaining amount to pay. A person may pay too much. Then four actions are taken. The outstanding amount is set to zero. The invoice is registered as paid. A credit note is created. The refund is done at once. An invoice may not be registered as paid. Then it can be modified. The inhabitant or the representative of the inhabitant may complain about the invoiced items. Then the items can be corrected.

A stay can end. This happens because of the death of the inhabitant. Or it happens because the inhabitant decides to move to another place. Then the invoicing continues. It continues until all invoices are paid. The type of the stay sets the amount that is invoiced. Here is an example. A service flat stay can go on. Meanwhile the inhabitant lives in rest & nursing for a while. In that case, both stays are invoiced. But a discount applies to the service flat stay.
