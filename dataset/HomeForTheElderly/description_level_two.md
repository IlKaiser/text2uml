A Home for the elderly is divided into departments. Each department consists of a number of rooms. Each room has a number of beds. Each inhabitant is assigned a bed for his or her stay. A bed belongs to a particular category. Each category has a price. The home offers different types of stays. These match the categories: rest home (RH), rest & nursing home (RNH), and service flat (SF). An inhabitant can have more than one stay at a time. For example, suppose John stays in a service flat. He breaks his leg. So he must stay in rest & nursing for a while. His stay in the service flat is then suspended. It is not ended. The switch to rest & nursing does not require a proposal. John is already an inhabitant.

People who wish to reside in the home go through a registration process. In this process, the person answers a set of questions. These questions concern his or her basic identification data. As a final step, the person's data is registered in the system. Then the basic registration is done. Next, the person's abilities are assessed. This is done through questions. The questions gauge the person's need for help with basic activities. These activities include eating, dressing, and personal care (like taking a shower). This assessment indicates the type of stay best suited for that person. The assessment results in a type of stay defined for the person. As soon as a person has been assessed, that person is placed on the waiting list. This is the waiting list for the corresponding type of stay. The position on the waiting list is determined by the registration date only. When a bed of the corresponding category is free, it is proposed to a person. This is the person at the highest position on the waiting list. That person must then inform the Rest & Nursing Home within three days. The person says whether he or she accepts or refuses the proposal. Suppose the proposal is refused. Then the person stays on the waiting list. A new proposal is made to the next person on the list who requested this type of stay. This continues until someone accepts the proposal. Once a proposal is accepted, the person's abilities are re-assessed. This validates the requested type of stay. After this validation, the date of intake is determined. The effective stay starts on that day. Sometimes the type of stay needs to be changed after the re-assessment. Then the proposal is marked as invalidated. The requested type of stay is updated. But the person keeps the same position on the waiting list.

Stays are invoiced once a month. For simplicity, payments are not treated as objects. They are treated as events. When a payment is received, it is registered. The outstanding amount of the invoice is reduced by the amount paid. If the remaining amount is zero, the invoice is registered as "paid". Sometimes the remaining amount is still positive. This means the paid amount was too low. Then the outstanding amount is adjusted. The invoice is re-sent. It indicates the remaining amount to pay. Sometimes a person pays too much. Then the following actions are taken. The outstanding amount is set to zero. The invoice is registered as paid. A credit note is created. The refund is executed immediately. As long as an invoice is not registered as paid, it can be modified. So the inhabitant or his or her representative may complain about the invoiced items. This can then be corrected.

A stay may come to an end. This can be because of the inhabitant's death. It can also be because the person decides to move elsewhere. Then the invoicing processes continue until all invoices are paid. The type of the stay determines the amount that is invoiced. For example, a service flat stay may continue while an inhabitant resides in rest & nursing for a while. Then both stays are invoiced. But a discount applies to the service flat stay.
